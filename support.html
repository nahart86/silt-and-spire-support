Studio basics

How do I pour sand?

Draw on the stone to guide a stream. In Studio menu, choose Freehand for direct placement or select Ring, Spiral, Turrets, or Rosette in Design. Hold and drag to pour; release to stop. A design guide steers where grains land—it does not place a finished shape.

Where are the colors and design settings?

Open Studio menu and choose Sand, Design, or Tools. Sand has six colors—Dune, Ivory, Clay, Basalt, Sea glass, and Rose—plus wetness and brush width. A grain keeps the color it had when poured, so you can layer colors into bands or patterns.

What do Pour, Carve, and Orbit do?

Pour adds grains using your selected placement or design guide. Carve scoops sand from a stroke to make channels and grooves. Orbit lets you drag to turn the camera around your sculpture without adding sand.

What does the Flow slider do?

Flow controls the amount of sand emitted during a pour on every device. On hardware that reports touch force, pressing harder can also adjust the stream. Otherwise, the game estimates changes from finger contact size; this is not measured pressure. The Flow slider remains available either way.

What does wetness change?

Wetness is a relative game control for the simplified grain-spreading and cohesion rules. Dry sand spreads more, a damp mix can hold steeper forms, and runny sand slumps more easily. It is not a measured water percentage.

What are Cove, Spire, and Ledge?

They are three foundations with different widths. Cove is broad, Spire is narrower, and Ledge is the most constrained. Each gives the same sand a different balance challenge.

Why did my sculpture lean or fall?

The balance indicator reflects support as the sculpture grows. Added weight can settle and compact grains below it; a shifted load or narrow section can lose support and topple. Best heights are stored locally, not on an online leaderboard.

How do I start again?

Choose New sculpture in Studio menu and confirm. The active sculpture clears, while saved studies and local best heights stay on your device. A topple ends the current attempt; start a fresh sculpture to try another design.

Does my active sculpture resume when I reopen the app?

No. Relaunching starts a new sculpture. Your saved studies and best local records remain available from the shelf.

Saved sculptures and sharing

Where are my saved sculptures and records?

They are stored on this iPhone in the app’s local storage. There is no account, server, cloud sync, or remote leaderboard. You can save a sculpture as a postcard and view or remove postcards from your shelf.

How does PNG sharing work?

Choose to export a postcard and iOS opens its share sheet. You choose whether to share and which destination to use. A destination you select may handle the image under its own privacy terms.

Will my saved work come back if I delete the app or change phones?

Saved work is kept locally and is not synced or backed up by Silt & Spire. Deleting the app removes its local data. We do not provide a restore service or guarantee that app data will transfer to another device.

Accessibility and controls

Can I place sand without drawing across the screen?

Yes. Open Studio menu → Tools and use the left/right and front/back placement sliders, then tap Add a pinch for a small pour. Flow, wetness, and brush width are adjustable controls; labeled controls and sliders support VoiceOver.

Does pressure control work on every iPhone?

No. Touch force is available only when the device and input report it. On other screens, changes in finger contact size are used as an estimate, not measured pressure. The Flow slider works on every device.

About the sand model

The scene is a stylized, reduced-order particle model. Grains fall under gravity and respond to nearby contacts, support, and simplified moisture-dependent cohesion. It is not a full fluid or engineering simulation; centimeter values are game scale.

AI and graphics

Generative AI tools assisted substantially with game design and code implementation. No image-generation bitmap assets or runtime AI are used. The interface and 3D scene are rendered with SwiftUI and Metal.
